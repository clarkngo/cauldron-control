How a shift works

1. Watch

Read the , the and the . Keep the brew between 65 and 85 °C. They do not always agree.

2. Notice

Something goes wrong: a bad sensor, an attacker, a flood of alarms, an AI that is too helpful. Work out which instrument to believe.

3. Decide

Act, or shut down. Then see a chart of what was true against what the screen showed, with the defences to discuss.

The shift, round by round

They are meant to be played in order. Pick any card to jump straight there, or add ?round=3 to the address.

What you will take away

A screen is a view

Know which instruments depend on the control system and which do not, and cross-check before you act.

Faults are not always attacks

Separate a failed sensor from a spoofed one, and weigh the cost of a needless shutdown.

Independence and limits

Segmentation, a separate safety system, a sane alarm list, and an AI with the least authority that does the job.

You cannot lose

Every round ends in a debrief whatever happens, and a score is feedback, not a gate. Each brief says what to aim for, and a Need a hint? button gives up to three nudges, from gentle to nearly the answer.

How scoring works
  • Brew completed: your yield (the share of ticks inside 65–85 °C), minus 3 per field check and 5 per vent.
  • Justified shutdown (true temperature at or above 85 °C): 70.
  • Safety system trips the brew: 35. Safe, but you were not in control.
  • False alarm (shutting down a safe brew): 20.
  • Blowup: 0.
  • A +10 convenience bonus applies if the brew completes with a flat network (round 4) or with the assistant left on autopilot (round 6).
